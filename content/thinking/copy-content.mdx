# Copy + Content

The team has writers, so copy is not the main deliverable. But the direction is important. And one thought governs all of it.

> Communication should promote calm, quiet confidence, even when not everything is known and not everything is done.

The guiding rule for *what* to say is always the next right step.

## The voice, as a filter

Run every line through this. Calm, warm, invitational, not directive. Plain sentences with quiet depth, first-person plural, sentence case. Normalize not-knowing, the signature move for this segment of the audience: you do not need to have it all figured out to begin well. Leave room to celebrate. Calm is the floor, not a ceiling: when a leader does something real, warmth can rise to meet it. No hype, no urgency, no guilt, no jargon. No em dashes. One ask per message. Name the action, never the AI.

## Two hierarchies

**Which one thing, across messages.** Say the single thing that most serves the leader now, in this channel.

- **Primary** is the one next right step. Always exactly one (as far as possible). Aimed at building quiet confidence.
- **Secondary** is the conversation, a quiet link to go deeper, never a competing ask.

**Within a single message:** inspiring the feeling, or disposition, is the primary aim, almost always carried by tone rather than stated. Then what to do, the single action, and only then what to know. Inspiring calm should arrive almost entirely implicitly, through the feel of the content, not as an explicit instruction.

## The skeleton

One warm line of acknowledgement, often celebratory. The one thing. A beat that lowers the weight or lifts them up, because it is small and finishable, or because they are more ready than they think. A single action. Then, quietly, the secondary. This shape governs the composed messages, like the email. The conversation does not follow it line by line, but it carries the same voice and the same one-next-step discipline, which is what keeps the whole experience feeling like one voice.

## How the conversation opens

The email's secondary link carries a message pre-filled as the leader, *"I would like to talk through getting ready for my first session."* So the conversation opens with the leader's own words already said, and responds to them. It never opens by greeting or introducing itself. The prefill can be tuned per email or segment, so different touchpoints open the conversation in a more tailored way.

## A canonical example

The first email, week one, where the primary aim is confidence:

> Hi Sarah,
>
> You've said yes to leading your group through the Practicing the Way Course. That is a meaningful, generous thing, and you don't need to have it all figured out to begin well.
>
> We'll take the next few weeks a step at a time, sending a note now and then to point you to one small, helpful thing.
>
> There's one thing worth doing first. Not inviting anyone yet, not planning sessions. Watching session one of the leader training, about 6 minutes, helps the shape of what's ahead feel familiar.
>
> **Begin session one.** Not sure where to start? *Talk it through.*
>
> Peace,
>
> Practicing the Way
