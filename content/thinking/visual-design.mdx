# Visual Design

The look should follow the brand, because the aesthetic is itself an argument for calm. Font at regular weight even for headings. Warm off-white surfaces by default, with pure white where the purpose calls for it. Near-black text. On-brand color used with restraint, no competing accents in a single context. Generous whitespace, soft diffuse shadows, motion that fades and rises rather than snaps.

> An invitation, not a notification.

It should read and feel like a premium editorial journal, not a tech product and not a church website.

## The surfaces

Three surfaces carry the experience. The piece built out in full is the coupled flow, the email into the conversation. The in-app surface is sketched as direction.

**The conversation.** Not a chatbot. No avatar, no robot glyph, no "AI is typing," never labelled "AI Chat." A calm reading column, messages set as warm typography with room to breathe, and a single quiet input field. Suggested next steps appear as cards, and delivered resources as inline cards. The whole thing is titled by the action, *"Prepare for your first session,"* never by the technology.

**The email.** A letter, not a campaign. A single column about 520px wide on warm off-white, a regular-weight headline, and two or three short paragraphs. One salmon button for the single next step, and beneath it one quiet text link onward into the conversation. Warmly signed. The restraint is the aesthetic.

**The in-app surface.** An invitation, not a notification. Singular, as far as possible: one element, one action, with nothing competing or stacking. Placed, not alarming: no badges, no red dots, no unread counts, no urgency. It only ever does one thing at a time: invite the immediate next step, or capture a single data point about how confident the leader feels. What makes it read as an invitation is the restraint, the placement, and the copy.

## In-app, in three intensities

The same invitation plays at three intensities, sketched here as direction for how it would live in the app.

- **Centered arrival.** For threshold moments, like a first visit. A moment worth pausing for. This submission uses it too: a contemplative pause holds one thought before each layer of thinking.
- **Inline card.** The everyday next right step on the workspace. Part of the room.
- **Corner invitation.** Ambient, low-stakes nudges. The next live session is Thursday. The lightest touch.

## The language rule

Name the experience by its function. Never personify it, never brand it as "an AI." The container is a conversation. The entry point is the action. The model underneath is plumbing the leader never needs to think about.
